FATAL
Wrong document class — this will be desk rejected at any journal
You are using \documentclass[conference]{IEEEtran} — the IEEE conference paper template. Computers in Biology and Medicine (your target journal) uses the Elsevier article class. IEEE Access uses IEEEtran journal mode, not conference mode. A submission with the wrong template signals to editors that the paper was never meant for that journal.
Fix: Change to \documentclass{elsarticle} for Elsevier journals. For IEEE Access use \documentclass[journal]{IEEEtran}.
FATAL
Paper has no figures — this alone will get a rejection
The entire paper has zero figures. Journal papers in computer vision require: (1) an architecture diagram as Figure 1, (2) preprocessing comparison showing before/after, (3) Grad-CAM++ heatmap examples as qualitative results, (4) a calibration reliability diagram, (5) training loss curves. Reviewers cannot evaluate your architecture from equations alone. A CV paper with no visual results is unpublishable.
Fix: You need minimum 4 figures. These must be created during experiments and inserted.

Technical and methodological gaps reviewers will flag
FATAL
Joint training strategy for two datasets with different labels is not described
APTOS images have DR labels only (no HR). HRDC images have HR labels only (no DR). You cannot compute both L_DR and L_HR on every batch. How do you train? Options: (A) Alternating batches — one batch from APTOS (only L_DR active), next batch from HRDC (only L_HR active). (B) Mixed batches with masking — HR loss is masked to zero for APTOS images, DR loss masked for HRDC. (C) Pseudo-labeling. You must pick one, implement it, and describe it explicitly with an Algorithm box. This is the most critical missing implementation detail in the paper.
Add Algorithm 1: Multi-dataset alternating training procedure. This is probably your cleanest option — two DataLoaders, one per dataset, alternated each batch, losses computed only on relevant labels.
MAJOR
FPN equation (Eq. 1) notation is ambiguous
Eq. 1 writes the FPN fusion in a single nested expression that's hard to follow. The "Lat" function is undefined formally. Separate it into 3 equations: P5' = Lat5(P5), P4' = Lat4(P4) + Up(P5'), P3' = Lat3(P3) + Up(P4'). Then F_out = BN(Conv3x3(P3')). This is standard FPN notation from Lin et al. 2017.
MAJOR
Vessel decoder architecture (Eq. 5) is underspecified
Eq. 5 writes V_pred = σ(Conv(Dec(P3,P4,P5))) — this tells the reader nothing about the decoder architecture. How many upsampling blocks? What channel sizes? What skip connection strategy? The decoder needs either a small table of layer specifications or a detailed paragraph. Conference papers can get away with vague descriptions; journals cannot.
MAJOR
DRIVE dataset is far too small for meaningful training (20 images)
DRIVE has only 40 images total (20 train, 20 test). Reviewers will immediately question whether meaningful vessel segmentation learning happens on 20 images. You need to either: (A) supplement with CHASE_DB1 (28 images) and STARE (20 images) to reach ~68 training images, or (B) use transfer learning from a pretrained vessel segmentation model, or (C) frame this as fine-tuning rather than training from scratch. Acknowledge this limitation explicitly.
MAJOR
No justification for N=30 MC Dropout passes
You state N=30 forward passes for MC Dropout but give no justification. Standard practice is to show an uncertainty convergence plot: as N increases from 5 to 50, does the uncertainty estimate stabilize? Show that N=30 is the point of convergence. Without this, a reviewer will write "the choice of 30 stochastic passes appears arbitrary."
MINOR
Uncertainty threshold of 0.15 is unjustified
u > 0.15 triggers specialist referral. How was 0.15 chosen? Plot sensitivity/specificity of the referral decision vs. threshold on a validation set, and pick the threshold at the operating point matching a clinical requirement (e.g., 95% sensitivity). This is standard for clinical decision thresholds.

Augmentation is listed in prose. Journal standard is a table: Transformation | Parameters | Probability. This makes it reproducible. Currently the paper says "elastic transforms" without parameters (alpha, sigma) — another researcher could not reproduce your setup.

Evaluation gaps — what journal reviewers always demand
FATAL
No SOTA comparison — papers cannot be accepted without this
Need a comparison table against published methods on APTOS 2019. Minimum 5 baselines. Include: EfficientNet-B0 (yours), ResNet-50 fine-tuned, VGG-19 fine-tuned, and 2-3 published papers from 2021-2024 that report QWK on APTOS. Even if MSDNet doesn't beat all of them on raw QWK, you can argue advantages in multi-task capability and uncertainty.
MAJOR
No statistical significance testing
Journals require that performance differences be statistically validated. When you report QWK = 0.91 vs baseline 0.82, you need to show this difference is statistically significant. Run 5-fold cross-validation and report mean ± std. Add a paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test comparing MSDNet vs baseline across folds. Without this, reviewers may argue the improvement is within noise.
MAJOR
Ablation table is missing key columns
Current ablation table has DR QWK, HR AUC, ΔQWK. Missing: (1) ECE column — shows if each component affects calibration, (2) Parameter count — shows efficiency at each step, (3) Training time per epoch — shows computational cost. Also, rows 1-3 show "---" for HR AUC because the HR branch doesn't exist yet in those configs — this should say "N/A (no HR branch)" to be clear.
MAJOR
No confusion matrix for DR grading
DR grading is a 5-class ordinal problem. QWK is your primary metric but a confusion matrix is essential to show WHERE misclassifications occur (grade 1 vs 2 vs 3 boundary confusion is clinically very different from grade 0 vs 4 confusion). Include as a figure or supplementary table.
MAJOR
Uncertainty analysis needs a reliability (calibration) diagram
ECE alone is a single number. A reliability diagram (confidence bins on x-axis, actual accuracy on y-axis, diagonal = perfect calibration) shows the full calibration picture. This is the standard visualization for uncertainty papers and must be included as a figure.
MINOR
Qualitative XAI results need to show failure AND success
The planned heatmap figure should show: (A) clean cases where DR and HR heatmaps are clearly separated (validating disentanglement), (B) a co-occurring case showing both heatmaps active in different regions, (C) a failure case where the model is wrong — show what the heatmap looks like on a wrong prediction. Showing only successes is not scientifically credible.

The discussion is where you show scientific insight, not just numbers.
